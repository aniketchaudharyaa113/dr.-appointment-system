# Emergency Triage & Red-Flag Symptoms
Source: Emergency Medicine Clinical Guidance / WHO Emergency Triage Assessment and Treatment (ETAT)
License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)

## Critical Emergency Indicators (Immediate Ambulance Dispatch Required)
1. **Cardiovascular / Chest**:
   - Crushing, heavy, or radiating chest pain (to jaw, neck, left arm, or back).
   - Sudden collapse, severe dizziness with palpitations, cold clammy skin.
2. **Neurological / Stroke (FAST)**:
   - Facial drooping on one side.
   - Arm weakness or numbness.
   - Slurred speech or difficulty finding words.
   - Sudden severe "thunderclap" headache or loss of consciousness.
3. **Respiratory Distress**:
   - Inability to complete a sentence without gasping.
   - Cyanosis (bluish tint around lips or fingernails).
   - Audible stridor, severe wheezing, or choking.
4. **Severe Trauma & Anaphylaxis**:
   - Severe uncontrollable hemorrhage / bleeding.
   - Severe swelling of throat/lips accompanied by hives and difficulty swallowing.
   - Major burns, active seizures, or ingestion of toxic substances.

## Protocol
In any of these presentations:
1. Do NOT delay care with digital appointments.
2. Direct caller immediately to Dial 112 (National Emergency) or 108 (Direct Ambulance).
3. Tap "Request Ambulance" on MediBook for automatic GPS dispatch to the nearest trauma facility.
